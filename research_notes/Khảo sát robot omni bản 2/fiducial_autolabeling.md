# Auto-labeling with fiducial markers and other automatic sources for markerless robot/object detection and pose estimation

Scope note: the target system attaches an ArUco marker to a small omni robot seen by an overhead webcam. The marker pose auto-labels bboxes and heading/keypoints, a YOLO-style model is trained, and then the marker is removed. Verification legend: **[V]** = details checked on the primary source page or PDF during this research. **[A]** = abstract or landing page only. **[U]** = from background knowledge, not re-verified in this session (the writer should treat it as unverified).

## Which works use fiducial markers to auto-label data and then remove the marker for deployment?

### Takeaway
Several published works use the "marker for labels, then hide the marker" pattern. Rosskamp et al. (WACV 2024) is the most directly relevant controlled study: markers on the object, LaMa inpainting, and a test set without markers. PACE (ECCV 2024) and DABACO (arXiv 2026) use ArUco markers in the scene or on a screen and then inpaint them out. In the robotics setting, ArUco-to-YOLO auto-labeling exists (Uemura 2025), but it detects the marker itself rather than going markerless.

### Cited Findings

**1. Rosskamp, Weller, Zachmann, "Effects of Markers in Training Datasets on the Accuracy of 6D Pose Estimation", WACV 2024, pp. 4457-4466 (CVF page range; researchr lists 4445-4454, a conflict) [V]** — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/html/Rosskamp_Effects_of_Markers_in_Training_Datasets_on_the_Accuracy_of_WACV_2024_paper.html); [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf); [code: 6DPoseDataGenTools](https://github.com/JHRosskamp/6DPoseDataGenTools)
- Labeling source: marker-based optical tracking (OptiTrack-style markers on the object) gives the 6D ground truth. The experiments mostly use synthetic BlenderProc renders where markers are rendered on the object, plus a real tea-box set ("a marker-based and a markerless set of 200 real images each", plus 59 markerless real test images) — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- Four training conditions: I, no markers (baseline); II, markers visible; III, markers removed by inpainting; IV, augmentation with 1-5 randomly placed synthetic markers on the object "to learn pose estimations invariant to markers"; V, half fixed markers and half augmented — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- Removal method: the pre-trained LaMa model (Suvorov et al.) "without any adaptation". The mask comes from projecting the known 3D marker positions and checking visibility with depth. The raw masks gave too many artifacts, so they dilate them 4x and 8x and inpaint twice ("double masking"). A few images still show artifacts and elongated marker shadows — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- Model: GDR-Net (the main model, 20,000 tea-box training images) and ZebraPose (a cross-check on YCB-V) — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- Results on the synthetic test set without markers (Table 2), as AUC ADD-S / AUC ADD-(S) / ADD-(S): I 97.1/90.0/84.8; II (markers visible) 96.2/87.8/**76.6**; III (inpainting) 97.0/90.0/**84.8**; IV 97.0/89.8/84.2; V 96.7/88.8/80.0. The conclusion says unedited markers "reduced the accuracy of predictions by 8 percent points w.r.t. ADD-(S)". The body text says "5.6 percent points", which does not match the table difference of 8.2. — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- Shortcut evidence: when the test set contains markers (Table 3), the model trained with markers scores best (ADD-(S) 87.1), and the markerless-trained model scores 77.7. The authors read this as "the neural network learns the markers as additional features for pose estimation" — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- Real data (Table 4, fine-tuning on 200 real images): markerless real images reach ADD-(S) 45.8 and inpainted real images reach 47.5, against 20.3 without fine-tuning. Inpainted real images "do not reduce recall". On YCB-V, I and III differ by about 1% on average. With ZebraPose, III is 0.6 pp lower on synthetic and 0.9 pp higher on real — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- Label-noise study: manual annotation of 37 frames against the exact GT gave Δt ≈ N(0.1, 2.2) mm, and many rotations were off by more than 2°. Perturbing the labels with σR = 2.5° lowers recall by 1.5-7 pp. Labeling errors above σR = 2.5° and σt = 2 mm, or σt = 4 mm alone, pull accuracy down to the "markers visible" level. Conclusion: "marker-based training data is superior to a markerless dataset, if pose labeling is not done very carefully" — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- Stated limitation and future work: test whether the results hold for real marker-based data and for "imprecise marker-object registration" — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)

**2. You, Xiong, Yang, Huang, Zhou, Shi, Fang, Harley, Guibas, Lu, "PACE: A Large-Scale Dataset with Pose Annotations in Cluttered Environments", ECCV 2024 (arXiv 2312.15130) [V]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15130); [ECCV PDF](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/06837.pdf)
- Size: 55K real frames, 258K annotations, 300 videos, 238 objects in 43 categories, plus PACE-Sim with 100K frames and 2.4M annotations. Calibrated 3-camera RealSense D415 rig — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15130)
- ArUco markers, placed on the **background** rather than the object, automate still-object pose annotation via PnP, "following LINEMOD and NOCS". Stated limitations: "training on marker-augmented imagery may result in overfitting to these artificial patterns", and marker annotation does not work for moving objects — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15130)
- Removal ("marker inpainting strategy"): Step 1 records the scene with Marker 1. Step 2 adds Marker 2 and records both. Step 3 removes Marker 1 and captures the real data with Marker 2 only. Frames from steps 1 and 2 show the surface under Marker 2, which is used to "seamlessly inpaint" Marker 2's area. In effect, real background pixels are reused instead of hallucinated ones. Marker 2 tracking is corrected by hand every 40 frames — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15130)
- Moving objects use BundleTrack plus a manual correction every 10 frames, the "most labor-intensive" part. Annotation accuracy, checked against NOCS-REAL275 scene 1: 0.9° rotation and 2.3 mm translation — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15130)

**3. Gómez-Cárdenes, Marichal-Hernández, Martín-Doñas, "DABACO: A Multi-Camera Dataset and Benchmark for Screen Localization and Pointing Estimation", arXiv 2610.03928v1, 2 Oct 2026 (preprint) [V]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03928v1)
- Four ArUco DICT_4X4_50 markers are embedded in the screen corners of the displayed video. A multi-pass detector labels anchor frames, bidirectional Lucas-Kanade optical flow fills the gaps, and a human verifies the result. Of 32,629 frames, 52% were labeled by direct detection, 43% by flow propagation, 1.5% by manual correction, and 3% were discarded — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03928v1)
- The markers and their quiet zones are removed with **Telea inpainting** at the GT corners "so models can't use them as trivial detection cues". The release includes the marked images, the clean images, and the masks — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03928v1)
- Stated limitation: inpainting can leave "directional streaks and dark boundary bleeding" that "might serve as shortcut cues". Labels were checked by a single annotator — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03928v1)
- Baselines: the classical pipeline reaches 92.63% coverage against 86.97% for YOLOv8s-seg (COCO, not fine-tuned). Mean IoU is 58.84% for YOLO and 52.98% for classical — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03928v1)

**4. Uemura, "About an Automating Annotation Method for Robot Markers", 2025, Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.17801449 (ISSN 2394-0840; journal not named on the page) [A]** — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/17801449)
- ArUco detections (ID and position) become YOLO labels automatically, in a RoboCup Logistics/factory setting. The YOLO model is claimed to beat OpenCV processing on blurred and defocused images. The marker stays in place at test time: the model detects the **marker**, not the robot. No numbers appear in the abstract — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/17801449)

**5. Semi-automatic labeling with AR markers: De Gregorio, Tonioni, Palli, Di Stefano, "Semi-Automatic Labeling for Deep Learning in Robotics" (ARS), arXiv 1908.01862, 2019 [V]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01862); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1908.01862v1)
- A tracked AR-marker pen defines the boxes once. A robot arm then moves a camera with known kinematics, and the boxes are projected into every frame. 9 sequences of about 35,000 frames took under 1 hour, against more than 10 h for 1,000 frames by hand (about 450x faster) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01862)
- The marker sits on the pen, not the object, so whether it appears in the images is not addressed — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1908.01862v1)

**6. AprilTags around the object, tags left in the images: "Seeing Glass: Joint Point Cloud and Depth Completion for Transparent Objects", arXiv 2110.00087 [A]**. Annotation is fully automated with AprilTag 36h11 (40 mm) placed around the transparent objects plus the known CAD model. The tags are not removed — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.00087)

**7. Invisible (UV-fluorescent) markers: Li, Guo, Tu, Ji, Liu, Ye, Zheng, "Textureless Deformable Object Tracking with Invisible Markers", arXiv 2308.13678v2 (29 May 2024; venue not shown) [V]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13678v2)
- UV dyes are invisible under normal light. A rig alternates UV and normal lighting: the UV frames give labels, and the normal-light frames are marker-free training images. The DOT dataset has about 200 sequences and about 1M marker-free frames. Limitations: a complex rig (42 cameras, 60 UV LEDs, FPGA triggering), misalignment during fast motion, and hand-drawn markers — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13678v2)

### Inferences
- The most-cited approach is the WACV 2024 controlled study. It shows that visible markers cost about 8 pp ADD-(S), and that LaMa inpainting fully recovers that loss (84.8 against 84.8). This is the strongest evidence that "auto-label with a marker, then inpaint" is sound. It was measured on object 6D pose with synthetic markers, not on robots or 2D detection.
- Most auto-labeling datasets (LINEMOD, NOCS, PACE, Seeing Glass) put markers on the background, not on the object. Background markers are a weaker shortcut than an on-object marker, because a detector can only use a background marker to locate the camera, not the target. The target system puts the marker **on the robot**, which matches the Rosskamp setting (on-object markers), where the shortcut is strongest.

### Gaps
- None of the verified works uses a marker attached to a **mobile robot** to label a later markerless robot detector.
- I could not retrieve the full text of Uemura 2025, so dataset size and metrics are unknown.

## How do they avoid the network learning the marker itself?

### Takeaway
Five strategies appear in the literature. (1) Inpaint with a learned model (LaMa) using masks from the known marker pose. (2) Inpaint from real background pixels recorded before the marker was placed (PACE two-marker trick). (3) Classical Telea inpainting (DABACO). (4) Augment with random synthetic markers so no single marker location carries information (Rosskamp IV/V). (5) Capture labels and images under different imaging conditions so the marker never appears in the training images (UV markers). Inpainting artifacts are a named residual shortcut risk.

### Cited Findings
- Using LaMa with dilated double masks matched the markerless baseline. Artifacts and long marker shadows remained in a few images. Random-marker augmentation reached 84.2 against 84.8, but it is "not feasible when real images are captured", so the authors suggest mixing synthetic random-marker images with real fixed-marker images (80.0). Inpainting was the best practical option — [Rosskamp et al., WACV 2024](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- Real-background inpainting: the area under the marker is captured before the marker is placed (Marker 1/Marker 2 protocol) and used to fill the marker region — [PACE, arXiv 2312.15130](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15130)
- Telea inpainting of the markers and their quiet zones, with masks released so others can use their own mask-based augmentation. Inpainting streaks are flagged as a possible shortcut — [DABACO](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03928v1)
- UV-fluorescent markers with alternating lighting: the training images are captured marker-free by design — [Li et al., arXiv 2308.13678](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13678v2)
- Related: learned blind marker removal in medical images, without a mask input — [arXiv 2303.15124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15124) [A]

### Inferences
- For the omni robot, a strategy not found in any paper is to use the **marker pose to define a mask** (marker corners plus a margin) and inpaint the robot's top plate there. Because the inpainted region sits *on* the robot, the inpainting has to make up robot texture, not background. This is harder than PACE's background case, and artifacts are more likely to become a cue.
- Lower-risk alternatives that follow from the cited principles: (a) a marker that matches the robot's top color, or one that is IR or UV-only (the Li et al. principle); (b) a **two-pass protocol**: record with the marker, then remove it and replay the same trajectories, which works if the robot's odometry repeats precisely (unverified feasibility for an omni robot with wheel slip); (c) mount the marker on a mast above the robot, or under a part the webcam does not see, so it is absent from the robot crop; (d) random marker pasting or cut-out augmentation over the marker region (Rosskamp IV, Cutout-style).
- Evaluation protocol worth borrowing from Rosskamp: always keep a **real test set without markers**, plus a sanity test with markers present, to measure shortcut reliance (the gap in Table 3).

### Gaps
- No paper found measures shortcut learning for **2D bbox detection or keypoint/heading** (as opposed to 6D pose) when an on-object marker is inpainted.
- No paper found studies the "remove the marker and replay known trajectories" approach for mobile robots.

## What accuracy gap is reported between auto-labeled and manually labeled training data?

### Takeaway
Automatic labels are usually as good as or better than manual labels when geometric precision matters. Manual 6D labels carry errors of several mm and degrees, which measurably hurt pose accuracy. For 2D detection, the evidence is mixed and depends on the detector.

### Cited Findings
- Rosskamp: manual 6D annotation error is Δt ≈ N(0.1, 2.2) mm, with 22 of 112 rotation samples off by more than 4°. Label noise of σR 2.5° and σt 2 mm drops accuracy to the level of training with visible markers. "marker-based training data is superior to a markerless dataset, if pose labeling is not done very carefully" — [WACV 2024 PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- ARS (De Gregorio et al.), mAP@0.5 on Industrial_Test+: YOLO trained on 1,000 manual images scores 0.589, against **0.731** with 1,000 ARS auto-labeled images. SSD scores 0.619 manual against 0.562 auto, so there is no uniform winner. The manually labeled test set favors avgIoU for manual training. Manual and ARS labels agree at precision 98.49%, recall 95.02%, avgIoU 0.7 (IoU 0.3). ARS boxes include occluded parts, while humans box only the visible part. The abstract claims "precision and recall … increased by about 15%", which is not found as numbers in the body — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1908.01862v1)
- DeepScanner (robot-arm data collection): reports +4.84% test precision and a 240x labeling speed-up compared with manual labels — [arXiv 2108.02555](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.02555v1) [A, via search snippet]
- PACE annotation accuracy: 0.9° and 2.3 mm — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15130)
- DABACO: 1.5% of frames needed manual correction after automatic marker labeling plus flow — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03928v1)

### Inferences
- For an overhead camera with a planar robot, ArUco corner localization is sub-pixel, so heading labels from the marker are probably more precise than hand-clicked heading keypoints. The main error source is the marker-to-robot-centre offset (the "marker-object registration" Rosskamp flags), which should be calibrated once.
- Box semantics differ: a box projected from geometry includes occluded or out-of-sight parts, as in the ARS finding. If the robot model is a known cylinder or prism projected from the marker pose, decide whether the boxes should be amodal.

### Gaps
- No study found reports auto-label vs manual-label mAP for **mobile robot** detection from an overhead camera.

## Self-supervised robot pose estimation using kinematics, encoders, mocap or CAD as supervision

### Takeaway
For manipulators this is a mature area. DREAM (ICRA 2020) uses synthetic data, CAD and domain randomization with PnP. CtRNet (CVPR 2023) adds a self-supervised sim-to-real step through differentiable rendering of masks, using joint angles. Surgical robots use kinematics to auto-label segmentation (ICRA 2019). For mobile robots, the closest is Bultmann et al. 2023, which uses synthetic renders for keypoints on the robot base seen by external cameras. Mocap projection is used to auto-label drone detectors.

### Cited Findings
- **DREAM**: Lee, Tremblay, To, Cheng, Mosier, Kroemer, Fox, Birchfield (author list [U] beyond "Timothy E. Lee et al."), "Camera-to-Robot Pose Estimation from a Single Image", ICRA 2020, pp. 9426-9432. It predicts 2D keypoint belief maps, then uses PnP with forward kinematics. It is trained **only on synthetic data with domain randomization**, tested on Panda, Kuka iiwa7 and Baxter. Single-frame accuracy is "close to classic offline hand-eye calibration". Code: NVlabs/DREAM (non-commercial licence) — [arXiv 1911.09231](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09231); [NVIDIA](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2020-03_DREAM) [A]
- **CtRNet**: Lu, Richter, Yip (UCSD), "Markerless Camera-to-Robot Pose Estimation via Self-supervised Sim-to-Real Transfer", CVPR 2023, arXiv 2302.14332. It has a segmentation plus keypoint network trained on synthetic data, then self-supervised on **unlabeled real images** by comparing a differentiable rendering of the robot mask (from known joint angles and the estimated pose) with the predicted segmentation. Claims state-of-the-art accuracy and is demonstrated in PBVS — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.14332); [CVPR poster](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2023/poster/21563) [A]. Follow-up: CtRNet-X for partially visible robots — [arXiv 2409.10441](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10441v1) [A]
- **Self-supervised surgical tool segmentation**: da Costa Rocha, Padoy, Rosa, "Self-Supervised Surgical Tool Segmentation using Kinematic Information", ICRA 2019, DOI 10.1109/ICRA.2019.8794334. Labels come from the robot kinematic model, with optimization to cope with unknown hand-eye calibration and imprecise kinematics. An FCN is fine-tuned. Tested on phantom and in-vivo flexible endoscopy data — [arXiv 1902.04810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.04810) [A]
- **Holistic robot pose with unknown states**: "Real-time Holistic Robot Pose Estimation with Unknown States", arXiv 2402.05655 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.05655) [A, title only; authors and venue not verified; probably Ban et al., ECCV 2024 [U]]
- **Mobile robot from external cameras**: Bultmann, Memmesheimer, Behnke (Univ. Bonn), "External Camera-based Mobile Robot Pose Estimation for Collaborative Perception with Smart Edge Sensors", arXiv 2303.03797, 2023 (venue not shown; possibly ICRA 2023 [U]). MobileDet finds the robot, and a MobileNetV3 keypoint network predicts 2D keypoints on the **robot base**. A 3-DoF (x, y, θ) pose comes from multi-view reprojection with Levenberg-Marquardt and is fused with odometry in a pose graph. **Labels**: 36k synthetic keypoint samples (stillleben renderer); detection uses 12k synthetic plus 3.5k manually annotated real images. Accuracy: 2.93 cm and 0.88° multi-view, 4.25 cm and 1.12° single-camera. RMS trajectory error falls from 18.5 cm (internal only) to 4.48 cm — [alphaXiv/arXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2303.03797)
- **Mocap auto-labeling of drones**: "YOLOv5 Drone Detection Using Multimodal Data Registered by the Vicon System" (Sensors 2023) projects Vicon 3D poses to 2D for "precise and automatic labeling of a real dataset". The Vicon markers on the drone are visible, and whether they are removed is not stated — [PMC10385035](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10385035/) [A]. A related multimodal indoor 3D drone tracking dataset uses Vicon to automate dataset preparation — [Sci. Data 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04521-y) [A]
- **YolPnP-FT** ("Real-Time 6D Pose Estimation and Multi-Target Tracking for Low-Cost Multi-Robot System", Sensors 2025, 25(23):7130) uses YOLOv8 keypoints plus PnP for small multi-robot systems, with a RealSense D435i and reportedly a top-down setup. Labeling source not verified, because both pages blocked access — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/23/7130); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12693832/) [A]
- Other background works (all [U], not re-verified in this session): RoboPose (Labbé, Carpentier, Aubry, Sivic, CVPR 2021, render-and-compare robot pose with unknown joints); LabelFusion (Marion, Florence, Manuelli, Tedrake, ICRA 2018, RGB-D reconstruction for mass labeling); Deng et al. "Self-supervised 6D Object Pose Estimation for Robot Manipulation" (ICRA 2020, robot interaction gives labels).

### Inferences
- An omni robot has no articulated joints, so "kinematics as supervision" reduces to the **robot's own odometry or IMU**, which drifts with wheel slip. A fixed overhead ArUco marker is a better label source than odometry. Odometry could be used to gap-fill labels between marker detections, the way DABACO uses optical flow.
- Bultmann et al. show that a CAD/synthetic route reaches about 1° heading and a few cm without markers. That is the best published reference point for the accuracy a markerless planar robot localizer can reach.

### Gaps
- Full author lists and venues for several entries (DREAM, Holistic, YolPnP-FT) were not fully verified.

## Is "ArUco-supervised markerless localization of a mobile robot from an overhead camera" already published?

### Takeaway
I found **no published work** that does exactly this: a marker temporarily attached to a mobile robot, an overhead camera, marker-derived bbox and heading labels, a detector trained on them, and the marker removed at deployment. The idea is assembled from published parts (marker auto-labeling with inpainting for object pose; external-camera keypoint pose for mobile robots; ArUco-to-YOLO labels), but the specific combination appears to be unpublished as of Nov 2026, within the limits of this search.

### Cited Findings (closest matches, with similarity ratings)
- **Rosskamp et al., WACV 2024**, similarity **high in method, low in domain**. It covers the on-object marker, auto-labels, inpainting and a markerless test, with quantified shortcut learning. But it uses 6D pose of household objects, mostly synthetic data, and GDR-Net rather than a YOLO detector on a robot — [PDF](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/wacv24/wacv24-paper.pdf)
- **Bultmann et al., 2023**, similarity **high in task, different in label source**. It covers a markerless mobile robot, external cameras, a detector plus base keypoints, and a 3-DoF (x, y, θ) pose. Labels come from synthetic renders plus manual annotation, not markers — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2303.03797)
- **Uemura 2025 (Zenodo)**, similarity **medium**. It covers robots, ArUco-to-YOLO auto-labels and RoboCup Logistics, but the marker stays in place and is the detection target, so it is not markerless — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/17801449)
- **PACE / DABACO**, similarity **medium in method**. ArUco auto-labels plus inpainting to prevent shortcuts, but the markers are on the background or screen, and the domain is not robots — [PACE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15130); [DABACO](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03928v1)
- **Low-cost ground robot with ArUco on top, bird's-eye camera, YOLOv8n/YOLOv11n on a Raspberry Pi 4** (ScienceDirect 2026, S277237552600122X), similarity **medium-low**. It uses an overhead view and a marker on the robot top, but YOLO detects the **marker** at runtime. Search snippets report near-perfect recall up to 1.5 m/s. Details are unverified because the page was not fetched — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S277237552600122X) [A]
- **RoboCup SSL**: the overhead camera tracks robots with colored top patterns (SSL-Vision). The neural-detector dataset (Fernandes, Rodrigues, Barros, RoboCup 2021) has 931 **manually** labeled images. A TIGERs thesis uses logged SSL-Vision positions to *evaluate* detection, but I found no work that uses them to *train* a detector that runs without the patterns — [arXiv 2106.14597](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.14597); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-98682-7_5); [TIGERs thesis 2024](https://download.tigers-mannheim.de/papers/2024-Vision-based-Understanding-of-the-RoboCup-Small-Size-League-field-Weinmann.pdf) [A]
- **Vicon-labeled drone detection (Sensors 2023)**, similarity **medium**. An external tracker auto-labels 2D boxes of a robot (drone) for YOLOv5, but the source is mocap rather than ArUco, the camera is not overhead, and markers are probably left in place — [PMC10385035](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10385035/) [A]

### Inferences
- A defensible novelty claim for the new system: "marker-supervised, markerless planar (x, y, θ) localization of a small mobile robot from one overhead webcam, with explicit handling of the on-robot marker shortcut". The claim should cite Rosskamp 2024 (method), Bultmann 2023 (task) and PACE/DABACO (inpainting practice) as prior art. Claiming novelty for "auto-labeling with fiducials" in general would be wrong.
- Experiments that would make the contribution credible: compare (i) training with the marker visible, (ii) marker inpainted, (iii) marker region masked or randomly pasted, and (iv) a small hand-labeled set. Test on footage of the robot **without** the marker, with the robot's true pose measured some independent way, for example a second marker on a mast that is out of the training crop, or a floor grid.

### Gaps
- Searches cannot cover non-English venues, student theses or small-conference papers comprehensively. A Google Scholar search for terms such as "ArUco" "auto-annotation" "mobile robot" "markerless", in English and other languages, is recommended before claiming novelty.
- Could not verify the full text of Uemura 2025, the ScienceDirect 2026 ground-robot paper, or YolPnP-FT (paywall, 403 or captcha).
