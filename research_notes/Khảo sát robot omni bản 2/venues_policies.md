## Q4. AI-assistance disclosure policies (IEEE, Springer Nature, MDPI, Elsevier)

### Takeaway
All four publishers forbid listing AI as an author and require disclosure of substantive AI use. They differ on where the disclosure goes: **IEEE** in the Acknowledgments, **Elsevier** in a separate declaration placed before the References, and **Springer Nature** as a description of use under its Green/Amber/Red risk scheme. **MDPI** exempts basic language editing from disclosure (secondary source).

### Cited Findings
- **IEEE**: AI-generated content "shall be disclosed in the acknowledgments section of any article submitted to an IEEE publication". Authors must name the AI system and identify the affected sections with a brief explanation. The fetched IEEE page does not explicitly address AI authorship. — [IEEE Author Center, Submission and Peer Review Policies](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/)
- A secondary source says IEEE recommends, but does not strictly require, disclosure of grammar-only editing. **[secondary]** — [manusights Journal AI Policies 2026](https://manusights.com/blog/journal-ai-policies-2026)
- **Springer Nature**: core principles include "AI may support but not replace scholarly judgement" and "human accountability cannot be transferred to AI systems". — [Springer Nature Editorial Policies](https://www.springernature.com/gp/policies/editorial-policies)
- Springer Nature's "AI use in manuscript preparation" policy uses Green, Amber and Red levels. "Listing AI as an author" is Red (not permitted). For Green and Amber uses, authors should "Clearly describe use and confirm author accountability". For Amber, "all AI generated or assisted content must be clearly labelled". Grammar and language editing is Green (low risk), but the page does not present it as exempt. AI-generated visuals "not derived from independently verifiable data… [are] not permitted". — [Springer Nature, AI use in manuscript preparation](https://www.springernature.com/gp/policies/editorial-policies/ai-manuscript-preparation)
- Conflict: older or secondary sources say Springer Nature does not require disclosure of AI-assisted copy editing and wants disclosure in the Methods section or Acknowledgements. The current policy page (above) frames disclosure as a description of use without that exemption. — [Springer Nature group AI guidance](https://group.springernature.com/gp/group/ai/ai-guidance-for-our-researchers-and-communities); [IOU LibGuide](https://libguides.iou.edu.gm/c.php?g=1482669&p=11059634)
- **MDPI**: generative-AI tools do not meet authorship criteria and cannot be listed as authors. Basic language editing (grammar, punctuation, spelling, formatting) is exempt from disclosure. **[secondary; mdpi.com/ethics returned 403]** — [CASRAI summary of MDPI policy](https://casrai.org/guides/mdpi-generative-ai-authorship-policy)
- **Elsevier**: AI tools "must never be used as a substitute for human critical thinking, expertise and evaluation". Permitted uses include "Improving language, clarity, and readability". Disclosure goes in "a separate AI declaration statement" placed "immediately before the references", headed "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process", with the template "During the preparation of this work, the author(s) used [NAME OF TOOL / SERVICE] in order to [REASON]." Authors "should not list AI tools as an author or co-author, nor cite AI tools as an author". AI must not create or alter primary data images, general-purpose generative AI must not be used for graphical abstracts, and AI-assisted explanatory images require disclosure in the caption. — [Elsevier Generative AI policies for journals](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals)

### Inferences
- For this project, where AI assistants were used for code and writing: in an IEEE venue (SMC, RO-MAN, IEEE Access), add an Acknowledgments sentence naming the tool and the sections or code it assisted. In Elsevier journals, use the declaration template. In Springer CCIS (SoICT, ROBOVIS), describe the use and confirm author accountability. Never list the AI as an author.
- Assisting with code is not addressed separately in the fetched IEEE text. The safest practice is to disclose it alongside writing assistance.

### Gaps
- The official MDPI ethics page could not be read, so MDPI's exact disclosure location (e.g. a Methods section or cover letter) is unverified.
- The IEEE conference-specific (Author Center for conferences) AI policy page returned 404. The journal-side page was used instead.

## Q5. Extending a conference paper into a journal paper

### Takeaway
It is allowed if you disclose the prior paper, cite it, and clearly state what is new. IEEE requires this explicitly. For MDPI Sensors special issues built on selected conference papers, the expectation is about **50% extension**. A "≥50% new content, ≤30% copy-paste" rule was found only on a non-MDPI conference page.

### Cited Findings
- **IEEE**: "Authors submitting articles must disclose whether there are prior publications, e.g., conference articles," and "must also include information that very clearly states how the new submission differs from the previously published work(s)". The prior work should be cited. — [IEEE Author Center, Submission and Peer Review Policies](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/)
- **MDPI Sensors** special issues of selected conference papers invite extended versions with "50% extensions of contents of the conference paper". The Eurosensors 2018 issue asked authors to expand to full research-article size. — [Sensors SI flyer](https://www.mdpi.com/journal/sensors/special_issue_flyer_pdf_v2/IXBLIF57OV); [Sensors SI SP15ICST](https://www2.mdpi.com/journal/sensors/special_issues/SP15ICST); [Sensors SI Eurosensors 2018](https://www.mdpi.com/journal/sensors/special_issues/eurosensors2018)
- The "minimum of 50% new content and not exceeding 30% copy/paste" wording comes from a third-party conference page, not MDPI policy. — [pixel-online MDPI journal selection](https://conference.pixel-online.net/FOE/MDPIjournalFOE.php)
- **SciTePress/Springer**: VISAPP plans a Springer CCIS book of "revised and extended papers" from the conference. — [VISAPP 2027 home](https://visapp.scitevents.org/home.aspx)

### Inferences
- A practical plan is to publish the conference version first (e.g. SMC 2027 in Apr 2027, or NICS/SoICT 2027), then submit an extended journal version (IEEE Access or Sensors). The journal version should add new experiments, for example more gestures, a user study, localization error analysis and comparison baselines. Aim for roughly 30–50% or more new material, and include a cover-letter statement of the differences.

### Gaps
- Springer Nature and Elsevier general policies on extended conference papers were not retrieved. These are often journal-specific.
- No official IEEE numeric threshold for new content was found. The IEEE policy is qualitative.
